Methods, definitions & downloads

Which series a number comes from

Retention and graduation appear on this page in more than one series, published by different offices over different universes. They are not interchangeable, and the gaps between them are larger than most of the trends they are used to describe. Every chart therefore carries a universe badge, and a series line sits directly beneath it.

A figure quoted without its series is ambiguous. “74.0%” is systemwide four-year graduation for the 2021 entering cohort, not Berkeley's, which is 80.6%. “About 92%” is systemwide first-year retention, not Berkeley's, which is about 96%. Retention is not graduation: first-year retention counts students enrolled at the start of the second year and says nothing about completion.

What kind of number is on the y-axis?

Measures are grouped in the picker by what they mean, and every chart carries a purple tag: % of entering cohort — the share of everyone who started (the usual "graduation rate"); % of graduates — a split among those who finished, saying nothing about how many finished; average of graduates — a mean (time to degree, final GPA) among those who finished; count — raw degrees conferred, not a rate; composition — who is enrolled or receiving aid, an input rather than an outcome; dollars — median earnings or debt. Where a true cohort share is not published, the page derives a transparent estimate and labels it "(est.)" with its denominator stated under the chart, rather than silently switching meanings.

Measures

Reading the by-campus completion panel

Federal C150 rates are keyed here to the Scorecard data year in which they were published. Each value reflects a cohort that entered roughly six years earlier; comparisons across campuses or groups within a year are unaffected, but do not read the x-axis as entering-cohort year. The amber x-axis tag restates this.

GPA bands

Bands are terciles of the systemwide enrollee GPA distribution for each cohort and entry type (weighted-capped high-school GPA for freshman entrants, transfer GPA for transfer entrants) — verified: the published band boundaries are identical across campus selections. Boundaries move year to year and are printed under the chart; each point's tooltip shows its cohort's band range. The lowest band always reaches down to that cohort's minimum recorded GPA (for the 2021 test-blind cohort this minimum is 0.00), so the chart reports only the informative inner boundaries. Because the bands are systemwide, campuses draw very unevenly from them: transfer sheets, which publish band rates alongside counts, imply that Berkeley's 2015 transfer entrants split roughly 200 / 520 / 1,450 across the three bands while Merced's split 73 / 12 / 10.

True band rates. The page now draws these views from a summary-API extraction of the same dashboard, which exposes the underlying full-precision band rates and their denominators (numerator ÷ rate; the harvest's internal consistency check agrees to ~10−12) for every campus × group × band × cohort cell, including per-campus and subgroup × band rates that the crosstab export left unidentified. Denominators are the dashboard's own universe — students with a usable GPA (systemwide ≈96% of the published entering cohort at 2019). Later windows are published as increments sharing the band denominator, so cumulative rates are exact sums. Rates for cohorts whose 5th/6th- year (transfer: 3rd/4th-year) windows haven't closed appear as real zeros in the source; those cohorts are omitted from the affected measures rather than plotted.

Maturity. A cohort's 5th/6th-year completions only exist once those years have passed. Freshman timing shares and six-year estimates are therefore shown only for cohorts old enough for the full window (through 2019 in the current data); later cohorts are omitted rather than plotted as artificial spikes. Transfer windows (2/3/4 years) are closed for all published cohorts.

Masking & small groups

School-level cells are shown by the source only when the entering cohort has at least 10 students, as integer percents. GPA-band timing shares are hidden here when fewer than 25 graduates underlie them. Systemwide lines carry the published cohort sizes in tooltips; small groups (for example American Indian entrants) are noisy — read them with their Ns.

Cohorts of 2020 and 2021

Entering cohorts 2020–2021 experienced pandemic disruption, and 2021 was the first test-blind cohort; 2020–21 high-school records also carry grade inflation. Charts shade these cohorts.

What these data cannot show

Individual students' grades, major switching, or reasons for leaving; anything about students below the published masking thresholds; and for Scorecard measures, anyone outside the federal-aid universe.

Data vintage

Admissions
UC Information Center, admission years through fall 2025.
Achievement
CDE CAASPP grade 11 (Smarter Balanced), spring 2015–2025. Spring 2020 was cancelled and spring 2021 is excluded as non-representative; each entering class is matched to its own grade-11 test the prior spring, so the 2021 and 2022 entering classes carry no CAASPP value.
UC outcomes
Entry cohorts 1999–2024. A rate appears only where its window has closed: six-year completion through the 2019 entering cohort, five-year through 2020, four-year through 2021, first-year retention through 2024.
Neighborhood
U.S. Census Bureau ACS 5-year estimates at census-tract level; ACS year set to the school year, with 2025 school years carrying the 2024 release.
